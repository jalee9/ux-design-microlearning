# AI-Assisted Presentation Development

Generative AI was used as a design partner in the development of the **Designing for the User** microlearning presentation.

The instructional content, learning outcomes, UX examples, live demonstrations, and final design decisions were instructor-developed and refined. AI tools were used to support brainstorming, iteration, and visual presentation design.

## Microsoft Copilot in PowerPoint

Microsoft Copilot was used to generate the initial visual design and layout for the PowerPoint presentation.

Rather than asking Copilot to create the instructional content, the prompt intentionally limited its role to **presentation design**. This allowed the visual design to support the lesson while keeping the instructional decisions instructor-directed.

### PowerPoint Design Prompt

> Create a visually polished PowerPoint presentation template for a short, interactive teaching demonstration on **User Experience (UX) Design** titled **“Designing for the User.”**
>
> Focus ONLY on the visual design, layout, and presentation theme. Do not generate, rewrite, or add instructional content. I will add all lesson content myself.
>
> The presentation will be delivered in person on a large conference-room screen and will alternate between PowerPoint slides and live website demonstrations. The visual design should therefore be clean, highly readable from a distance, and sophisticated without being distracting.
>
> Design direction:
>
> - Modern, human-centered UX/design aesthetic
> - Professional and appropriate for a higher-education leadership audience
> - Clean, contemporary, and visually engaging rather than corporate or overly academic
> - Generous whitespace
> - Strong visual hierarchy
> - Large, highly readable typography
> - Subtle visual references to digital interfaces, wireframes, user journeys, interface components, or web design
> - Minimal decorative elements
> - Avoid generic stock photography, people using laptops, futuristic AI imagery, circuit-board graphics, and cliché technology visuals
> - Avoid overly bright colors, gradients, excessive animation, or busy backgrounds
> - Use a restrained, sophisticated color palette with strong contrast and accessibility in mind
>
> Create layouts appropriate for:
>
> 1. A strong opening/title slide
> 2. A learning outcome slide
> 3. A scenario/challenge slide that can create a sense of urgency
> 4. A teaching/concept slide suitable for presenting three short questions or principles
> 5. A minimal transition slide for moving into a live website demonstration
> 6. A strong closing/takeaway slide with space for a QR code
>
> The slides should feel like parts of the same visual system while allowing each type of slide to have a distinct purpose.
>
> The overall presentation should visually reinforce the lesson itself: **good design should make information easy to understand and help the user know where to focus.** The template should model strong UX principles through clarity, hierarchy, consistency, readability, and intentional use of space.
>
> Do not populate the slides with lesson content beyond temporary placeholder text needed to demonstrate the layouts.

## From AI Output to Final Design

Copilot's output served as a **starting point rather than a finished presentation**.

The generated layouts were reviewed and adapted to support the specific learning experience. Content, hierarchy, wording, instructional sequencing, speaker notes, and live website interactions were refined to align the presentation with the lesson's learning outcomes.

This process demonstrates one approach to using generative AI in instructional development:

> **Use AI to accelerate creation while keeping instructional judgment, context, and decision-making human-centered.**
